## Direct Answer
Overall ranking by evidence strength and feasibility for BUB1B loss-of-function / mosaic variegated
aneuploidy (MVA):
1) WEE1 inhibitors, 2) HSP90 inhibitors, 3) IGF-1R inhibitors, 4) senolytics [3] [4] [13] [21] [22] [34] [36].
If the question is narrowed to which repurposed drugs most directly target aneuploidy-induced
proteotoxic stress, the mechanistic order changes to:
1) HSP90 inhibitors, 2) senolytics only in biomarker-confirmed senescent/SASP-high tumors, 3) WEE1
inhibitors, 4) IGF-1R inhibitors [1] [8] [9] [12] [29] [31].
The best actionable option today is WEE1 inhibition, because adavosertib has real pediatric and adult
clinical experience and repeated combination activity, especially where replication stress or TP53/DDR
vulnerability is present [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]. But that is an indirect CIN strategy, not a direct exploitation of
proteostasis collapse [1] [42].
The best mechanistic proteostasis strategy is HSP90 inhibition, since aneuploid cells have impaired
HSF1/HSP90 buffering and HSP90 inhibitors collapse multiple client pathways, including AKT and
IGF1R [8] [9] [10] [11]. The limitation is translational: pediatric trials showed minimal objective
responses and meaningful liver, ocular, and QTc toxicity concerns [13] [14] [16] [17] [18].
IGF-1R inhibitors are not strong aneuploidy-directed repurposing candidates. They have pediatric
rhabdomyosarcoma relevance, but single-agent efficacy was poor and no retrieved evidence links
BUB1B/SAC deficiency to IGF-1R dependence [12] [21] [22].
Senolytics remain the most speculative option. Aneuploidy can induce senescence and cGAS-
STING/SASP signaling, but SAC deficiency does not uniformly do so, and no direct BUB1B/MVA or
pediatric RMS senolytic studies were retrieved [27] [28] [30] [31] [34]. Navitoclax-class
thrombocytopenia is a major pediatric barrier [35] [36] [38].
Confidence level: High - the ranking is strongly supported by the provided synthesis and concordant
source patterns, though direct BUB1B/MVA-specific drug-testing evidence is absent [3] [12] [13] [28].

## Full Report
Clinical and Biological Context
## Foundational Context
BUB1B encodes BubR1, a core spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) component. Loss or mutation
impairs mitotic checkpoint control, causing chromosome missegregation, aneuploidy, developmental
abnormalities, and cancer predisposition characteristic of mosaic variegated aneuploidy [52] [53] [54]
[55]. In mouse and human systems, checkpoint failure is linked to chromosomal instability (CIN), and
perturbation of SAC genes such as BubR1, Bub1, and Mad2 can promote aneuploidy and tumor
formation, although the tumor effect is context dependent rather than universally oncogenic [56] [57]
## [58] [59] [60].
This matters therapeutically because aneuploidy is not just a karyotypic abnormality; it also creates a
reproducible cellular stress state. Whole-chromosome imbalance increases sensitivity to protein
synthesis and folding stress, alters stress-response programs, and increases reliance on protein-
quality-control systems including proteostasis and proteasome-related functions [44] [61] [62] [63].
That creates a plausible rationale for repurposed drugs that exploit stress-handling dependencies
rather than a lineage oncogene alone [9] [44].
At the same time, BUB1B deficiency can drive p53 activation, apoptosis, premature senescence, DNA
damage, and inflammatory signaling, but these outcomes vary by model and genetic context [27] [28]
[64] [65]. That heterogeneity is central to treatment choice: some strategies require a senescent state,
whereas others require active proliferation and replication stress [29] [30] [31].
Confidence level: High - the SAC-to-aneuploidy-to-stress framework is directly supported by multiple
foundational studies [44] [56] [62].
Genotype, phenotype, and cancer predisposition
BubR1 insufficiency in mice causes progressive aneuploidy, progeroid features, infertility, and
senescence phenotypes, showing that reduced BubR1 affects both genome maintenance and
organismal aging-like biology [43]. In neural tissue, BubR1 deficiency triggers chromosomal segregation
defects, DNA damage, p53 activation, and apoptosis, but only partial rescue by Trp53 co-deletion
indicates that non-p53 death pathways also operate [64]. Lower BubR1 levels can also induce p53/p21-
dependent senescence, while p53 loss permits highly aneuploid proliferation [27]. Similar p53
dependence is seen after MAD2 depletion in fibroblasts: cells with intact p53/p21 undergo premature
senescence, whereas INK4A/ARF-deficient backgrounds tolerate aneuploidy without strong
senescence [28].

Taken together, these findings suggest that BUB1B/MVA tumors may not share a single dominant
druggable state. Some may be proteostasis-stressed and proliferative; others may be damaged and
senescent; still others may bypass arrest if p53 pathways are compromised [27] [28] [29]. That is why a
biomarker-led rather than class-led strategy is more defensible [50] [65].
Confidence level: High - the evidence for heterogeneous outcomes after SAC failure is consistent
across several systems [27] [28] [30] [64].
Why Aneuploid Cells Are Drug-Sensitive
Proteotoxic stress and chaperone dependence
Aneuploid human cells show impaired HSF1 and HSP90 function, and HSF1 is described as a key factor
mitigating aneuploidy-associated proteotoxic stress [8]. Independently, aneuploidy is associated with
sensitivity to protein-folding stress and increased dependence on proteostasis and ubiquitin-
proteasome functions [44] [61] [62] [63]. HSP90 itself stabilizes client proteins, and its inhibition
redirects destabilized clients toward proteasomal degradation [66] [67].
Broader stress pathways reinforce this logic. ER stress activates the UPR via PERK, IRE1, and ATF6, and
the response can shift from adaptive to apoptotic depending on stress severity and duration [49] [68]
[69] [70] [71] [72]. Autophagy is often a protective arm of this response, although it may fail or become
suppressed as stress progresses [49] [72] [73] [74]. Interference with mitochondrial HSP90 can also
activate AMPK, inhibit mTORC1, induce autophagy, and trigger ER UPR, illustrating how chaperone
failure propagates into multi-system stress programs [75].
The key limitation is translational specificity: no retrieved paper directly measured these proteostasis
variables in BUB1B-deficient or MVA cells [12] [45]. So the therapeutic rationale is strong, but still
extrapolative.
Confidence level: High for the general aneuploidy-proteostasis model; Low-to-medium for direct
applicability to BUB1B/MVA because direct measurements are missing [8] [12].
Senescence, apoptosis, and inflammatory signaling
Aneuploidy and SAC disruption can produce senescence, but not uniformly. Reduced BubR1 and MAD2
depletion can induce p53/p21-linked senescence [27] [28], and whole-chromosome instability after
BUB1 or SMC1A reduction is associated with DNA breaks, oxidative stress, and a SASP-like phenotype
[65]. Yet BUB1B/BubR1 or Bub3 loss and Mps1 inhibition did not produce widespread senescence in
another timeframe/model, indicating that SAC bypass alone may be insufficient [30].

Inflammatory signaling provides a second layer of vulnerability. Micronuclei and cytosolic DNA can
activate cGAS-STING, which drives interferon and SASP programs [31] [32] [50] [76] [77]. Persistent
DNA damage signaling through ATM, NBS1, and CHK2 can sustain inflammatory cytokine secretion
including IL-6 without depending on p53 or pRb for the secretory output itself [78]. This supports the
idea that some highly unstable cells may transition into a senescent, inflammatory, potentially
senolytic-sensitive state [31] [79].
However, no retrieved study shows that SASP intensity, cGAS-STING activation, or senescence markers
predict drug sensitivity in BUB1B-deficient cells [29] [30] [50]. Therefore senolytic use without
biomarkers would be empiric rather than evidence-based.
Confidence level: High for heterogeneity of senescence biology; Low for using it as a selection
biomarker in BUB1B/MVA today [29] [30].
HSP90 Inhibitors
Mechanistic rationale and preclinical activity
HSP90 inhibitors are the most directly aligned with the proteotoxic-stress hypothesis. Aneuploid cells
have impaired HSF1/HSP90 buffering [8] [9], and HSP90 inhibitors destabilize multiple oncogenic
client proteins at once, including AKT and IGF1R, while inducing client degradation, apoptosis, or G2/M
arrest across tumor models [10] [11] [80] [81] [82] [83] [84] [85]. This multi-client effect is especially
attractive in genetically plastic CIN tumors, where single-pathway inhibition is often bypassed [11] [81]
## [83].
Preclinical pediatric solid-tumor work showed HSP90 inhibitors could deplete AKT and IGF1R and
combine greater-than-additively with cisplatin [10]. Other combinations, such as PU-H71 plus
bortezomib in Ewing sarcoma models, further support the idea that proteostasis-directed
combinations may outperform monotherapy [86].
A useful inference from the provided data is that HSP90 inhibition may partly subsume IGF-1R
targeting, because IGF1R is among the affected client pathways [10] [51]. Inferred: this makes
standalone IGF-1R blockade less compelling in BUB1B-related disease when the aim is broad stress-
network collapse rather than one receptor pathway. Confidence level: Low - this is a cross-source
inference, not a direct comparative trial result [10] [51].
Pediatric rhabdomyosarcoma and solid-tumor data
The main translational weakness is efficacy. Pediatric phase I studies of 17-AAG in recurrent/refractory
solid tumors reported no objective responses; one trial identified an MTD of 270 mg/m² with DLTs
including grade 3 transaminitis and hypoxia, while another recommended 360 mg/m²/day without DLTs
in the evaluated schedule [13] [14]. Pharmacodynamic evidence confirmed target engagement,
including Hsp70/Hsp72 induction and assessment of Akt changes [13] [14]. In pediatric preclinical
testing, AT13387 showed only modest single-agent activity and no objective xenograft responses [15].

Thus, HSP90 inhibition has mechanistic strength but limited pediatric proof of efficacy [13] [15]. The
better near-term use case appears to be rational combinations rather than monotherapy [10] [86].
Toxicity and feasibility in children
Toxicity is a major constraint. HSP90 inhibitors have documented hepatotoxicity/liver enzyme
elevation, ocular toxicity including retinal dysfunction and blurred vision, QTc prolongation, and
common diarrhea [16] [17] [18] [19] [20]. Pediatric pharmacology reviews also emphasize that children’s
developing organs increase ADR vulnerability and justify long-term follow-up for growth and organ
effects [7].
For a constitutional CIN syndrome such as MVA, the provided evidence does not prove excess
syndrome-specific toxicity, but it equally does not exclude it [7]. That uncertainty lowers feasibility.
Confidence level: High for mechanism and toxicity profile; Medium for translational ranking because
efficacy data are limited and indirect for BUB1B/MVA [8] [13] [17].
WEE1 Inhibitors
Mechanistic rationale in chromosomal instability
WEE1 inhibition does not directly attack proteotoxic stress. Instead, it amplifies replication stress, DNA
damage, checkpoint failure, and mitotic catastrophe, which can be useful in CIN-high or TP53/DDR-
vulnerable tumors [1] [2] [5] [42] [87]. In practical terms, this makes WEE1 a strong strategy for the
consequences of SAC deficiency rather than the proteostasis defect itself [1] [42].
Adult studies support this mechanism repeatedly. Adavosertib showed activity with carboplatin in
platinum-refractory ovarian cancer and demonstrated target engagement in multiple DNA-damage
combination settings [1] [2] [88] [89]. Preclinical work also found synergy with 5-FU, cytarabine, and
HDAC inhibition, with increased DNA damage and apoptosis [42] [87] [90]. PTEN loss and DNA-PK-
related vulnerability were linked to greater sensitivity in one breast cancer study, showing that
biomarker-defined subsets may matter [5].
Pediatric and adult clinical activity
Among the four classes evaluated, WEE1 has the strongest pediatric translational signal. Pediatric
trials of adavosertib with irinotecan reported objective responses or prolonged stable disease in
relapsed neuroblastoma and other solid/CNS tumors [3] [4] [91]. Adult combination studies
additionally support feasibility with gemcitabine-radiation and platinum regimens [1] [92].
This is why WEE1 ranks first overall: not because it is the best match to proteotoxic stress, but because
it has the best existing bridge from mechanism to clinic [1] [3].

Safety limitations and missing CIN-specific data
The main cited toxicities are myelosuppression and diarrhea [6]. Pediatric safety data remain limited,
and no retrieved study addressed constitutional CIN syndromes specifically [7]. No direct BUB1B/MVA
model testing was found [12], and no provided study directly examined WEE1 plus ATR inhibition in this
context [93].
So WEE1 is the most actionable option, but with an important caution: in a developmentally vulnerable
child with constitutional chromosomal instability, DNA-damage-intensifying regimens may risk
nonmalignant tissue injury, and the provided literature does not resolve that risk [6] [7].
Confidence level: High for ranking WEE1 first overall; Medium for applying it to MVA specifically
because the evidence is indirect [1] [3] [12].
IGF-1R Inhibitors in Pediatric Rhabdomyosarcoma
Monotherapy outcomes
IGF-1R inhibitors are clinically familiar in pediatric sarcoma, but efficacy as monotherapy is poor. R1507
in recurrent/refractory RMS, osteosarcoma, and synovial sarcoma produced a 2.5% objective response
rate and very short median PFS, while pediatric RG1507 studies reported no objective responses
though some patients achieved stable disease beyond 12 weeks [21] [22]. In recurrent/refractory
sarcoma, only one RMS partial response was reported, and some tumor shrinkage in RMS was transient
and lasted under 4 weeks [21]. Reviews across pediatric sarcoma trials consistently describe only
modest or disappointing activity [25] [26] [94].
Resistance mechanisms and combination logic
The weakness of IGF-1R monotherapy is biologically understandable in the provided evidence.
Resistance can arise via RTK redundancy and bypass signaling, including IRS1/PI3K/STAT3/p38 MAPK
upregulation and other compensatory pathways [95] [96] [97]. In RMS models, synergy has been
reported with HER2 inhibition, Src-family/YES inhibition, PDGFRα blockade, and ALK inhibition [46]
## [98] [99] [100] [101].
However, none of these RMS studies explicitly modeled chromosomal instability or aneuploidy-driven
biology [101]. That sharply limits their relevance to BUB1B/MVA-specific repurposing.
Pediatric safety and BUB1B-specific gaps
Pediatric and mixed-age studies describe IGF-1R antibodies as generally tolerable but with metabolic,
hematologic, GI, and constitutional toxicities; combination cohorts reported diarrhea and neutropenia
as DLTs in some settings [21] [22] [102]. Later reviews note lack of survival benefit alongside increased
adverse events, but the provided text does not enumerate endocrine-growth toxicities in detail [23].
Crucially, no retrieved paper linked SAC deficiency, MVA, or BUB1B loss to altered IGF-1R/INSR/PI3K-
AKT signaling [12] [48] [103] [104] [105].

Senolytics and Senescence-Targeting Strategies
Senescence biology in aneuploidy and SAC deficiency
Aneuploidy can induce senescence and pro-inflammatory SASP via micronucleus-driven cGAS-STING
activation [31] [32] [47]. SAC depletion studies also support senescence-like programs in some
contexts [28] [33] [65]. Yet this state is not universal: senescence varies with p53 status and model
system, and SAC bypass alone may fail to generate widespread senescence [29] [30]. This means
senolytics are only biologically plausible in a subset.
Evidence for senolytic combinations
The “one-two punch” concept is supported in broader oncology: chemotherapy or radiotherapy
induces senescence, followed by senolytics such as navitoclax, FOXO4-DRI, or dasatinib plus quercetin
to remove senescent residual cells [34] [106] [107] [108] [109]. This is translationally interesting
because therapy-induced senescent cells may contribute to recurrence, metastasis, and bystander
effects through SASP [108] [109].
But the evidence gap is decisive: no retrieved study directly tested senolytics in BUB1B-deficient, SAC-
deficient, pediatric solid-tumor, or rhabdomyosarcoma models [110] [111] [112]. So the approach is
conceptually attractive but experimentally immature for this use case [34].
Toxicity and pediatric feasibility
Navitoclax and related BCL-XL-directed senolytics have clear platelet liability, with thrombocytopenia
repeatedly described as dose-limiting; neutropenia is also reported [36] [37] [38] [40] [113] [114] [115].
Dasatinib carries hematologic dysfunction, fluid retention, QT prolongation, arrhythmia/palpitations,
and rare ventricular dysfunction or heart failure [36] [39] [40]. Pediatric use is further limited by sparse
safety data and concerns regarding vulnerable organs and developing immunity [7] [35].
New platelet-sparing BCL-XL degraders are being developed [38] [116] [117], but these are better
viewed as future design solutions than present repurposing answers for MVA.
Confidence level: High that senolytics are the most speculative and least feasible current option [34]

The field is limited by a model mismatch: mechanistic aneuploidy studies largely use generic cell
systems and mouse models, whereas drug studies come from broader oncology rather than

Direct drug testing in BUB1B-loss/MVA models [12].

## Conclusion
For BUB1B loss-of-function MVA, the most defensible current translational choice is WEE1 inhibition if
the immediate priority is clinical feasibility, particularly in combination regimens and replication-
stress-prone tumors [1] [3]. If the priority is to target aneuploidy-induced proteotoxic stress itself,
HSP90 inhibition is the strongest mechanistic option, but present pediatric efficacy and toxicity data
are not yet persuasive enough for confident syndrome-directed use [8] [9] [13] [17]. IGF-1R inhibitors
remain lineage-relevant but biologically unconvincing as an aneuploidy-directed strategy [12] [21].
Senolytics should currently be viewed as hypothesis-generating tools for biomarker-selected settings
