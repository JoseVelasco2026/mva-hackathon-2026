




Evidence-based drug repurposing candidates for chromosomal instability (CIN)
syndromes like MVA1/BUB1B-related disease remain preclinical or early-phase;
none has been tested specifically in a child with biallelic BUB1B loss-of-function
mutations, and standard COG/EpSSG rhabdomyosarcoma chemoradiotherapy protocols
remain the evidence-based backbone of oncologic treatment.[1] The candidates below
exploit the biology of spindle-assembly-checkpoint (SAC) deficiency and aneuploidy-
driven proteotoxic/replication stress, but should be considered investigational, ideally
within a clinical trial or compassionate-use framework with close multidisciplinary
oversight given the patient's underlying genomic instability disorder.

Agents to flag as potentially problematic in this SAC-deficient background: standard
spindle poisons (vincristine, vinblastine) remain part of guideline-based RMS therapy
and should not be withheld, but there is a theoretical mechanistic concern that further
SAC perturbation on top of germline BUB1B deficiency could amplify chromosome
missegregation in surviving normal tissue; this has not been demonstrated clinically and
is not a basis to alter standard-of-care dosing without trial data. Investigational HSP90
inhibitors with significant hepatotoxicity should be avoided if combined with
hepatotoxic components of RMS chemotherapy (e.g., dactinomycin) outside a
monitored trial setting.[5]


Overall, the WEE1 inhibitor adavosertib and KIF18A inhibitors have the strongest
mechanistic and preclinical rationale for exploiting CIN/SAC deficiency specifically,
while HSP90 inhibition has the longest-standing but clinically unproven rationale for
aneuploidy-selective proteotoxicity.[2][3][7][4] Given the absence of pediatric or
MVA-specific trial data for any of these agents, use outside of standard
rhabdomyosarcoma protocols should occur only through a clinical trial, compassionate-
use pathway, or genomic tumor board with informed multidisciplinary and family
discussion, alongside the cancer surveillance recommendations specific to MVA1
patients.[1]
