## 1. Introduction
For spindle assembly checkpoint deficiency caused by biallelic BUB1B loss, the strongest repurposing rationale for
targeting aneuploidy-induced proteotoxic stress is HSP90 inhibition, because aneuploid cells consistently show
heightened dependence on chaperone-mediated proteostasis and repeated preclinical sensitivity to HSP90
blockade (Amponsah & Storchová, 2025; Oromendia & Amon, 2014; Kawakami et al., 2019; Simonetti et al., 2018).
This fits the biology of SAC failure, where chromosome missegregation creates stoichiometric imbalance in protein
complexes, aggregation pressure, increased protein degradation demand, and chronic stress-response
dependence (Ben-David & Amon, 2019; Simonetti et al., 2018; Amponsah & Storchová, 2025; Cyran & Zhitkovich,
## 2022).
The translational picture is less uniform than the mechanistic one. HSP90 inhibitors have the clearest direct link to
aneuploid proteotoxic vulnerability, but older pan-HSP90 agents were limited by toxicity and weak monotherapy
efficacy in many solid-tumor trials, whereas newer selective agents such as pimitespib/TAS-116 and biomarker-
guided strategies appear more viable (Rastogi et al., 2024; Zarguan et al., 2026; Sanchez et al., 2019). WEE1
inhibition has meaningful human oncology evidence, but it targets replication-stress and checkpoint dependence
more than proteotoxic stress itself (Di Rorá et al., 2020; Thangaretnam et al., 2025). Senolytics are attractive
because weakened SAC states and aneuploidy can drive senescence, yet the evidence is mostly indirect, model-
specific, and toxicity-limited, especially for navitoclax-class agents (Silva & Bousbaa, 2022; Kirkland & Tchkonia,
2020; Carpenter et al., 2021; Rad & Grillari, 2023).

## 2. Methods
This Deep Search synthesis evaluated research on drug repurposing strategies for aneuploidy-induced
proteotoxic stress in spindle assembly checkpoint-deficient disease contexts, especially BUB1B/BubR1
deficiency, mosaic variegated aneuploidy, chromosomal instability, and aneuploid cancers. The search ran over
more than 220 million research papers in Consensus, spanning Semantic Scholar, PubMed, and other indexed
sources.
The workflow identified 2,537 citation-expanded papers in addition to large targeted search returns, screened 230
papers after machine-learned relevance filtering, judged 123 unique papers eligible after deduplication, and
included the top 100 most relevant papers for final synthesis. Priority was given to human studies, clinical trials,
preclinical cancer models, and mechanistic papers published from 2010-2026 that directly addressed HSP90
inhibitors, WEE1 inhibitors, senolytics, autophagy/proteostasis targeting, or aneuploid stress biology.

3.3 HSP90 and WEE1 Evidence
HSP90 inhibition repeatedly emerges as the most mechanistically matched strategy because aneuploid cells are
more dependent on folding, degradation, and stress-buffering systems, and multiple reviews converge that 17-AAG
shows enhanced selectivity for aneuploid cells relative to euploid controls (Kawakami et al., 2019; Simonetti et al.,
2018; Oromendia & Amon, 2014; Saleh et al., 2020; Ben-David & Amon, 2019). This is especially relevant to mosaic
variegated aneuploidy, for which the literature explicitly notes the absence of direct MVA proteotoxicity testing but
highlights that complex aneuploid states should be investigated for the same vulnerability (Oromendia & Amon,
## 2014).

Human HSP90 trials are broad but disappointing as unselected monotherapy: 22 inhibitors across 186 trials
produced limited single-agent activity, frequent suspension or termination, and recurrent toxicity signals (Rastogi et
al., 2024; Liang et al., 2024; Cools et al., 2025). The more encouraging signals come from combination therapy,
proteostasis-burdened tumors, and newer selective compounds. Multiple myeloma is the clearest example, where
combined HSP90 and proteasome inhibition can collapse protein homeostasis and produced clinical responses
with tanespimycin plus bortezomib (Zarguan et al., 2026; Trepel et al., 2010; He & Hu, 2018). TAS-116/pimitespib
stands out because it is cytosolic-selective, has less ocular toxicity, has shown partial responses in phase I testing,
and reached regulatory approval in refractory GIST, which is the strongest clinical validation of HSP90 targeting in
the corpus (Chatterjee & Burns, 2017; Sanchez et al., 2019; Rastogi et al., 2024; Zarguan et al., 2026).
WEE1 inhibition is promising for tumors with checkpoint addiction, TP53 loss, or replication stress, and
adavosertib is the best-developed agent (Di Rorá et al., 2020; Van Bijsterveldt et al., 2020; Thangaretnam et al.,
2025). Clinical activity is real, especially in gynecologic cancers, where response rates of adavosertib monotherapy
or combinations ranged from 23% to 43% with median PFS 3.0-9.9 months, but toxicity and biomarker uncertainty
remain important constraints (Schutte et al., 2023; Kong & Mehanna, 2021). For rhabdomyosarcoma and other
pediatric solid tumors, replication-stress targeting is considered promising, but the strongest pediatric evidence in
the corpus favors ATR/CHK1/PARP/WEE1 as a broader class rather than SAC-deficient RMS specifically (Keller et
al., 2021).
3.4 Senolytics and Missing IGF-1R Evidence
Senolytics are biologically relevant because weakened SAC function, BubR1 insufficiency, and aneuploidy can drive
premature senescence, linking SAC deficiency to a senescence-clearance strategy (Silva & Bousbaa, 2022).
Within the queried options, navitoclax/ABT-263 is the best-established senolytic, and 17-DMAG provides an
HSP90-linked senolytic bridge, but senolytic effects are heterogeneous across models and not universal (Kirkland
& Tchkonia, 2020; Rad & Grillari, 2023; Carpenter et al., 2021; Saleh et al., 2020).

## 4. Discussion
The literature supports a clear ranking by mechanistic fit, not by proven disease-specific efficacy. HSP90
inhibitors best match the stated mechanism because they directly exploit the folding and proteostasis burden
caused by aneuploidy, and selective killing of aneuploid cells by 17-AAG is one of the few findings reproduced
across multiple reviews of the primary screens (Kawakami et al., 2019; Simonetti et al., 2018; Oromendia & Amon,
2014; Saleh et al., 2020). The weakness is translational: most HSP90 trials were not designed around aneuploidy or
SAC deficiency as biomarkers, so negative results in unselected populations do not fully test the specific
hypothesis relevant here (Rastogi et al., 2024; Zarguan et al., 2026; Magyar et al., 2023).
WEE1 inhibitors have better present-day human efficacy data than HSP90 inhibitors, but the mechanism is more
indirect for proteotoxic stress. They exploit replication stress, checkpoint dependence, and forced mitotic entry,
which could be relevant in BUB1B-deficient cancers but does not specifically target the proteostasis burden
created by aneuploidy (Di Rorá et al., 2020; Du et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2021). The biomarker story is also
incomplete: TP53, CCNE1, RAS, and replication-stress signatures help, but no paper here establishes BUB1B loss as
a validated predictor of WEE1 response (Schutte et al., 2023; Van Bijsterveldt et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024).
Senolytics are promising mainly as adjuncts if SAC deficiency produces a substantial senescent compartment in
tumor or normal tissues. That logic is plausible from BubR1 aging models and therapy-induced senescence work,
but cancer senolysis remains heterogeneous, and navitoclax-class thrombocytopenia is a serious translational
barrier (Silva & Bousbaa, 2022; Carpenter et al., 2021; Rad & Grillari, 2023; L'hôte et al., 2022). For personal
treatment decisions in a child with MVA syndrome and rhabdomyosarcoma, this evidence base is too indirect to
guide therapy without specialist review in pediatric oncology and clinical genetics.

## 5. Conclusion
Across this 2010-2026 corpus, the most defensible answer is that HSP90 inhibitors are the leading repurposing
candidates for aneuploidy-induced proteotoxic stress in spindle assembly checkpoint deficiency, with WEE1
inhibitors as the strongest clinically active adjacent class rather than the most mechanism-matched one.
Senolytics are promising mainly for a senescence-clearance strategy, and IGF-1R inhibitors lack direct support in
this literature.
## Research Gaps
The biggest gap is the absence of studies directly testing these agents in BUB1B-deficient or mosaic variegated
aneuploidy models, despite repeated calls to study how complex aneuploid states handle proteotoxic stress in vivo
(Oromendia & Amon, 2014; Amponsah & Storchová, 2025).
